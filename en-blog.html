How to write a motivation letter that works

For many programmes, the motivation letter is the most important part of your application after your grades, especially for degrees with limited places. Here's how to write one that really stands out.

Why does it matter so much?

Many degrees have a numerus fixus (limited places) and their own selection procedure. When two candidates have similar grades, the letter is often what makes the difference. It isn't a formality: it's your chance to persuade.

A structure that works

A good letter is usually between 500 and 800 words (always respect the programme's word limit) and clearly answers three questions:

  • Why this programme? Be specific: courses, approach, research areas.
  • Why this university? Avoid generic arguments and find out what makes it different.
  • Why you? Show who you are, not just what you've done, and connect your story to your goal.
From experience The weakest letters start with “I have always dreamed of…” or “My name is X and I want to study…”. Open with something specific that makes you memorable from the first line.

Mistakes that cost you a place

  • Copying sentences from the internet or other letters.
  • Sending the same letter to every programme (it shows).
  • Talking only about academic achievements without showing your personality.
  • Going over the word limit.
  • Not asking anyone for feedback before sending it.

The process we recommend

  1. Research the programme thoroughly before writing a single word.
  2. Write a first draft with no limit, including everything that comes to mind.
  3. Cut it down to the word limit.
  4. Ask for feedback from someone who doesn't know you too well.
  5. Leave it for 48 hours and review it with fresh eyes.

How much it costs to study in the Netherlands in 2026

Not as expensive as you think, and not as cheap as people say. These are the real numbers for living and studying in the Netherlands, and the support that can bring them down.

Tuition fees

If you're an EU citizen you pay the statutory fee (wettelijk collegegeld), the same as a Dutch student: €2,694 in 2026-27 at public universities and universities of applied sciences. For 2027-28 the fee has already been set at €2,771. Non-EU students pay a much higher institutional fee, set by each university.

You can borrow to pay your tuition As an EU student you can apply to DUO for a loan to cover your tuition fee (collegegeldkrediet), even if you don't work in the Netherlands.

* Dutch health insurance is mandatory if you work in the Netherlands (for example, to receive DUO student finance) and costs from around €150 a month; zorgtoeslag can give you back up to €129. If you only study, the European Health Insurance Card is usually enough. Rent varies a lot by city: Amsterdam and Utrecht are the most expensive.

The support that can reduce your costs

  • DUO basic grant (basisbeurs): about €325 a month in 2026 if you don't live with your parents. It becomes a gift if you graduate within 10 years.
  • DUO supplementary grant (aanvullende beurs): up to €491 a month, depending on your parents' income, on the same terms.
  • DUO loan: you can also borrow on favourable terms to cover your remaining costs.
  • Zorgtoeslag: up to €129 a month in 2026 towards your health insurance.
  • Huurtoeslag: only for self-contained housing with its own kitchen, bathroom and front door. Most rooms in shared houses don't qualify.
The requirement few people know about To receive DUO student finance as an EU student, you need to work in the Netherlands for an average of at least 32 hours a month. Many students meet it with a weekend job.

Sources: DUO, 2026 student finance amounts; DUO, requirements for EU students; Staatscourant, statutory fees for 2026-27 and 2027-28. Zorgtoeslag amounts are the 2026 figures published by Dienst Toeslagen.

DeadlinesUpdated October 20265 min read

Application timeline for starting in September 2027

Dutch deadlines are strict: one day late can cost you your place. These are the key dates for the 2027-28 academic year.

  • Sep – Oct 2026Research programmes and universities, finalise your shortlist and start preparing your motivation letter.
  • 1 October 2026Studielink, the official application platform, opens for 2027-28.
  • Nov – Dec 2026Request recommendation letters if required, gather your academic records and have them translated if needed. Book a date for your English test.
  • 15 January 2027Deadline for numerus fixus degrees (23:59 Dutch time). You can apply to a maximum of two per year.
  • 15 April 2027Numerus fixus programmes announce their selection results.
  • 1 May 2027Usual deadline for all other degrees if you're an EU student. Some programmes close earlier, so always check their website.
  • Jul – Aug 2027Find accommodation, open a bank account, register with the municipality to get your BSN and apply for benefits.
  • Sep 2027The academic year begins. Introduction week usually takes place just before.
Don't wait until the last day Studielink can slow down in the days before a deadline. Submit your application at least 10 days early.
